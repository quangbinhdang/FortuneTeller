### 1A) The 言→象→意 Hermeneutic in Practice

Wang Bi's tri-level hermeneutic (文辞→卦象→义理) is articulated in his 《周易略例·明象》:

> **Original Chinese:**
> 「夫象者，出意者也；言者，明象者也。尽意莫若象，尽象莫若言。言生于象，故可寻言以观象；象生于意，故可寻象以观意。意以象尽，象以言著。故言者所以明象，得象而忘言；象者所以存意，得意而忘象。犹蹄者所以在兔，得兔而忘蹄；筌者所以在鱼，得鱼而忘筌也。」

> **English:** "Images emerge from meaning-intent; words clarify images. Nothing exhausts meaning like images; nothing exhausts images like words. Words are born from images — hence one can trace words to observe images. Images are born from meaning — hence one can trace images to observe meaning. Meaning is exhausted by images; images are made manifest by words. Therefore: words serve to clarify images; once the image is grasped, release fixation on words. Images serve to preserve meaning; once the meaning is grasped, release fixation on the image. Just as the snare exists for the rabbit — once the rabbit is caught, forget the snare; the trap exists for the fish — once the fish is caught, forget the trap."

**Key doctrine distilled:**
- This is NOT an invitation to abandon text. It is a disciplined ascent: **text → image → principle**.
- Each level is necessary but instrumental — the goal is the principle (意/义理), not the textual surface.
- This anti-literalism preserves textual study while refusing to reify the literal surface.

### 1B) 乾卦 Commentary — The 卦主 System Applied

Wang Bi reads 乾 through his 卦主 (hexagram-ruler) framework, established in 《周易略例·明彖》. The hexagram-ruler is the single line that determines the hexagram's meaning:

**卦主 principles (from 《明彖》):**
1. **中位原则 (Central Position Principle):** The line at position 2 or 5, which occupies the center of the lower or upper trigram
2. **多寡原则 (Majority-Minority Principle):** In a hexagram with five yang and one yin, the single yin is the ruler; with five yin and one yang, the single yang is the ruler

**For 乾卦 — all six lines are yang, so the 多寡原则 does not apply. Wang Bi reads 乾 through the successive stages of yang energy, with each line representing a phase:**

> **Original (Wang Bi's 乾卦注, reconstructed from 《周易正义》 quoting Wang Bi):**
> 「《文言》备矣。」

Wang Bi's treatment of 乾 primarily follows the 《文言传》, emphasizing the ethical cultivation dimension of each line. His distinctive contribution is reading the line progression as a philosophical arc from latency to excess:

| Line | Wang Bi's Interpretive Emphasis | Key Concept |
|------|-------------------------------|-------------|
| 初九 潜龙勿用 | Yang qi not yet manifest; the noble person awaits the proper time | 待时 (awaiting the time) — NOT inability but strategic patience |
| 九二 见龙在田 | Virtue now visible; attracts the great person's recognition | 德施周普 (virtue's influence spreads universally) |
| 九三 终日乾乾 | Self-cultivation at the most dangerous position (neither central nor extreme) | 居上不骄，在下不忧 — neither arrogance above nor anxiety below |
| 九四 或跃在渊 | The threshold — advance or retreat, both valid | 进退无恒 — neither advance nor retreat is fixed |
| 九五 飞龙在天 | The ruler at the proper position; all under heaven well-governed | 大人造也 — the great person's creative achievement |
| 上九 亢龙有悔 | Excess of yang; the noble person knows when to yield | 知进退存亡而不失其正 — knowing advance/retreat/survival/perishing without losing correctness |

**Wang Bi's distinctive contribution to 乾:**
- Reading the line progression not as fortune-telling but as a **phenomenology of action**: when to act, when to wait, when to retreat
- The "fully explains human affairs" (全释人事) method — every line is read through the lens of human social-moral positioning, never through cosmological numerology

### 1C) 坤卦 Commentary — Yielding as Mastery

> **Wang Bi's 坤卦注 (reconstructed):** 「坤贞之所利，利于牝马也。马在下而行者也，而又牝马，顺之至也。至顺而后乃亨，故唯利于牝马之贞。」

> **English:** "What Kun's correctness benefits — it benefits the correctness of a mare. The horse is one that travels below [carrying], and specifically a mare — this is the ultimate of compliance. Only after attaining the ultimate of compliance can there be penetration — hence it only benefits the correctness of a mare."

**Key interpretive moves:**
1. **牝马 (mare)** is read philosophically, not literally — the mare symbolizes "leading by following," active receptivity rather than passive submission
2. Wang Bi emphasizes 坤's distinctive mode of agency: NOT weakness but the power that comes through yielding (柔能克刚 — the soft overcomes the hard)
3. The hexagram text "先迷后得" (first lost, then found) is interpreted as the natural rhythm of yin action — yin does not initiate but perfects what yang begins

**Contrast with Zhu Xi on 坤:**
- **Wang Bi:** Reads 牝马 as a philosophical symbol of compliant effectiveness; 坤 is about the DAO of yielding
- **Zhu Xi:** Reads it through the 筮占 lens — the diviner who receives this hexagram should understand that yielding and following is the auspicious path in this situation; the mare is a divinatory image for concrete guidance

### 1D) 复卦 — "复其见天地之心乎" — Wang Bi's Seminal Interpretation

This single line from the 《彖传》 has been one of the most debated passages in Yi Jing history. Wang Bi's interpretation established the dominant philosophical reading for nearly a millennium.

> **The line from 《复·彖传》:**
> 「复，其见天地之心乎！」

> **English:** "Return! Herein is seen the heart-mind of Heaven and Earth!"

> **Wang Bi's interpretation (reconstructed from scholarly consensus and 《周易正义》):**
> 「复者，反本之谓也。天地以本为心者也。凡动息则静，静非对动者也；语息则默，默非对语者也。然则天地虽大，富有万物，雷动风行，运化万变，寂然至无，是其本矣。故动息地中，乃天地之心见也。若其以有为心，则异类未获具存矣。」

> **English:** "Return' — this means returning to the root. Heaven and Earth take the root as their heart-mind. Whenever movement ceases, there is stillness — but stillness is not the opposite of movement. Whenever speech ceases, there is silence — but silence is not the opposite of speech. Thus, although Heaven and Earth are vast, rich with the ten-thousand things, with thunder stirring and wind moving, transforming without end — their silent, absolute non-being [寂然至无] is their root. Therefore, when movement rests within the earth [as in 复卦: thunder below, earth above], this is precisely when the heart-mind of Heaven and Earth becomes visible. If they took 'being' [有] as their heart-mind, then different kinds of things could not all obtain their existence."

**Key interpretive moves in Wang Bi's reading:**

1. **天地之心 = 无 (Non-Being / the Root):** Wang Bi identifies the "heart-mind of Heaven and Earth" NOT with activity, generation, or life-force — but with the still, silent root (寂然至无) from which all activity arises. This is pure 玄学 (Neo-Daoist metaphysics).

2. **Return (复) = Return to the Root (反本):** The one yang line at the bottom of 复卦 represents NOT just the return of yang energy after winter, but the philosophical return to the ontological ground of all existence.

3. **Stillness is not opposed to movement:** Wang Bi's signature dialectical move — 静 is not the opposite of 动; it is the ground that makes movement possible. This resolves the apparent paradox: how can a hexagram showing the RETURN of yang movement reveal the STILL heart of Heaven and Earth? Because the return IS the return to stillness — and only in returning to stillness does the yang energy find its source and renew itself.

**Historical impact:**
- This interpretation dominated Chinese Yi Jing scholarship for ~700 years (through the Tang-Song official curriculum)
- It established the Neo-Daoist reading where 无 (non-being) is ontologically prior to 有 (being)
- **Cheng Yi** later explicitly challenged this reading (see cross-school matrix below)

### 1E) 临卦 — The Approach of Yang

> **Wang Bi's 临卦注 (reconstructed):** 「阳转进长，阴道日消。君子日长，小人日忧。大亨以正之义。」

> **English:** "Yang turns and advances, growing; the yin way daily recedes. The noble person daily expands; the petty person daily worries. This is the meaning of 'greatly penetrating through correctness.'"

**卦主 analysis:** 临卦 has two yang lines at the bottom (初九, 九二) — applying the 多寡原则, the two yang lines represent the RETURNING/GROWING force pushing upward against four yin lines. Wang Bi reads this as the political-moral ascent of the noble person and the corresponding decline of petty influence.

**Key structural reading:**
- 初九 and 九二: the two advancing yang lines are the "rulers" of the hexagram
- The name 临 (approach/oversee) is read as the noble person's approach to govern
- 九二 is the "成卦之主" (constitution ruler) because it occupies the central position of the lower trigram — the most authoritative of the advancing yang

### 1F) Wang Bi's Method Summarized: The 三层次 Hermeneutic

| Level | Term | Chinese | Function | Wang Bi's Rule |
|-------|------|---------|----------|---------------|
| Level 1 | Text (Words) | 言 | The literal hexagram and line statements | "寻言以观象" — trace words to observe images |
| Level 2 | Image (Symbol) | 象 | The trigram patterns, line positions, yin-yang configurations | "寻象以观意" — trace images to observe meaning |
| Level 3 | Meaning (Principle) | 意 | The philosophical-moral truth disclosed | "得意而忘象" — grasp meaning, then release fixation on images |

**What Wang Bi did NOT do:**
- He did NOT abandon the text — his method requires disciplined textual study at Level 1
- He did NOT reject images — images are the necessary bridge from text to meaning
- He did NOT make meaning arbitrary — meaning is IN the images and text, not projected onto them

**What distinguishes Wang Bi from Han commentators:**
- **Han commentators (郑玄, 荀爽, etc.):** Meaning derived from external correspondences — hexagrams map to constellations, calendrical cycles, musical pitches, etc. (外在性 — externality)
- **Wang Bi:** Meaning derived from internal structure — the hexagram's own trigram dynamics, line positions, yin-yang relations explain its meaning (内在性 — internality). This is "全释人事" (fully explains through human affairs) vs. "多参天象" (largely consults celestial phenomena)

### 2A) 比 Bǐ — Holding Together / Union (Hexagram 8)

**Structure:** 坎 (Water) above, 坤 (Earth) below

> **卦辞:** 比：吉。原筮，元永贞，无咎。不宁方来，后夫凶。
> **English:** Bi: Auspicious. Examine the divination: supremely and eternally correct — no blame. The restless regions come [to submit]; the one who comes last: inauspicious.

> **《彖传》:** 比，吉也。比，辅也，下顺从也。原筮元永贞无咎，以刚中也。不宁方来，上下应也。后夫凶，其道穷也。

> **《大象》:** 地上有水，比。先王以建万国，亲诸侯。

**Line Text with English Translation:**

| Line | Chinese | English | Key Pattern |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| 初六 | 有孚比之，无咎。有孚盈缶，终来有它，吉。 | With sincerity, hold together — no blame. With sincerity filling the earthen vessel, in the end there comes the unexpected — auspicious. | Sincerity as foundation of union |
| 六二 | 比之自内，贞吉。 | Holding together from within — correctness: auspicious. | Internal unity, not forced externally |
| 六三 | 比之匪人。 | Holding together with the wrong people. | Warning: union with unworthy |
| 六四 | 外比之，贞吉。 | Holding together with those outside — correctness: auspicious. | Alliance with the worthy beyond one's circle |
| 九五 | 显比。王用三驱，失前禽，邑人不诫，吉。 | Manifest union. The king uses the three-sided hunt — the game ahead escapes. The city folk are not warned — auspicious. | The 九五 yang is the hexagram-ruler; voluntary union, not forced capture |
| 上六 | 比之无首，凶。 | Holding together without a head — inauspicious. | Union without leadership collapses |

**Interpretive note:** 比卦's single yang line at 九五 draws all five yin lines toward it — the quintessential 卦主 hexagram. The king (九五) unifies through virtue, not coercion ("三驱" — hunting on three sides, leaving one side open so game may escape voluntarily). This is Wang Bi's "一卦五阴而一阳，则一阳为之主" in perfect illustration.

### 2B) 小畜 Xiǎo Xù — Small Restraint / Gentle Taming (Hexagram 9)

**Structure:** 巽 (Wind) above, 乾 (Heaven) below

> **卦辞:** 小畜：亨。密云不雨，自我西郊。
> **English:** Xiao Xu: Penetrating. Dense clouds, no rain — from our western suburbs.

> **《彖传》:** 小畜，柔得位而上下应之，曰小畜。健而巽，刚中而志行，乃亨。密云不雨，尚往也。自我西郊，施未行也。

> **《大象》:** 风行天上，小畜。君子以懿文德。

**Line Text with English Translation:**

| Line | Chinese | English | Key Pattern |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| 初九 | 复自道，何其咎？吉。 | Returning to one's own way — what blame? Auspicious. | Return to proper path |
| 九二 | 牵复，吉。 | Drawn back to return — auspicious. | Returning with assistance |
| 九三 | 舆说輹，夫妻反目。 | The carriage's axle-strap comes loose; husband and wife turn eyes away. | Tension under restraint |
| 六四 | 有孚，血去惕出，无咎。 | With sincerity — blood departs, fear leaves — no blame. | The single yin (六四) is the hexagram-ruler; its sincerity resolves danger |
| 九五 | 有孚挛如，富以其邻。 | With sincerity binding — richness through one's neighbor. | Shared prosperity through trust |
| 上九 | 既雨既处，尚德载。妇贞厉，月几望，君子征凶。 | Rain has come, rest has come — virtue is carried. The wife's correctness: danger. The moon nearly full — the noble person marching: inauspicious. | The restraint completes; excess now dangerous |

**Structural note:** 小畜 has one yin line (六四) holding five yang lines in restraint. The image "dense clouds, no rain" captures the tension: accumulation without release. Cheng Yi classifies this under type (2) of his seven-fold typology — "取一爻之义" (meaning drawn from one defining line).

### 2C) 履 Lǚ — Treading / Conduct (Hexagram 10)

**Structure:** 乾 (Heaven) above, 兑 (Lake) below

> **卦辞:** 履：履虎尾，不咥人，亨。
> **English:** Lü: Treading on the tiger's tail — it does not bite the person. Penetrating.

> **《彖传》:** 履，柔履刚也。说而应乎乾，是以履虎尾不咥人亨。刚中正，履帝位而不疚，光明也。

> **《大象》:** 上天下泽，履。君子以辨上下，定民志。

**Line Text with English Translation:**

| Line | Chinese | English | Key Pattern |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| 初九 | 素履往，无咎。 | Plain treading forward — no blame. | Simple, unadorned conduct |
| 九二 | 履道坦坦，幽人贞吉。 | The path of conduct — smooth and level. The recluse: correctness — auspicious. | Withdrawal preserves integrity |
| 六三 | 眇能视，跛能履，履虎尾，咥人，凶。武人为于大君。 | Blind yet claiming to see, lame yet claiming to tread — stepping on the tiger's tail, it bites. Inauspicious. A warrior acting for the great sovereign. | 六三 is the hexagram-ruler — warning against presumption in the wrong position |
| 九四 | 履虎尾，愬愬，终吉。 | Treading on the tiger's tail — cautious, fearful — in the end, auspicious. | Same danger as 六三, but with awareness = different outcome |
| 九五 | 夬履，贞厉。 | Decisive treading — correctness: danger. | Even correctness can be dangerous when too rigid |
| 上九 | 视履考祥，其旋元吉。 | Examine the conduct, investigate the signs — turning back: supreme auspiciousness. | Retrospection and course-correction |

**Wang Bi's卦主 reading:** 六三 is the "成卦之主" — the single yin line treading among yang. Its position is dangerous (不中不正 — neither central nor correct), yet its quality of yielding (柔) enables it to navigate tiger territory. The hexagram is about the ethics of navigating power structures.

**帛书 variant (Mawangdui silk):** The silk manuscript uses **禮** (ritual/propriety) for the hexagram name instead of **履** (treading). This is a significant semantic shift — the silk tradition may have emphasized the ritual dimension of proper conduct rather than the metaphorical "treading."

### 2D) 泰 Tài — Peace / Prosperity (Hexagram 11)

**Structure:** 坤 (Earth) above, 乾 (Heaven) below

> **卦辞:** 泰：小往大来，吉，亨。
> **English:** Tai: The small departs, the great arrives. Auspicious. Penetrating.

> **《彖传》:** 泰，小往大来，吉亨。则是天地交而万物通也，上下交而其志同也。内阳而外阴，内健而外顺，内君子而外小人。君子道长，小人道消也。

> **《大象》:** 天地交，泰。后以财成天地之道，辅相天地之宜，以左右民。

| Line | Chinese | English | Key Pattern |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| 初九 | 拔茅茹，以其汇，征吉。 | Pulling up reed-grass — by its connected roots. Marching: auspicious. | Collective advance of the good |
| 九二 | 包荒，用冯河，不遐遗，朋亡。得尚于中行。 | Embracing the wild — crossing the river, not neglecting the distant, companions lost. Attaining honor through central conduct. | Inclusive governance; the central yang in the lower trigram |
| 九三 | 无平不陂，无往不复。艰贞无咎。勿恤其孚，于食有福。 | No level without a slope, no going without a return. Hardship in correctness: no blame. Do not worry about sincerity — in sustenance there is fortune. | The turning point — aware of inevitable reversal |
| 六四 | 翩翩，不富以其邻，不戒以孚。 | Fluttering downward — not enriched by neighbors, not warned by sincerity. | Yin descending to meet yang |
| 六五 | 帝乙归妹，以祉元吉。 | King Yi gives his younger sister in marriage — thereby blessed: supreme auspiciousness. | The sovereign yielding to alliance |
| 上六 | 城复于隍。勿用师。自邑告命，贞吝。 | The city wall collapses back into the moat. Do not use the army. From the city, issue commands — correctness: regret. | Tai reaching its limit; peace turning to stagnation |

**Interpretive significance:** 泰 represents the ideal cosmic state — Heaven (乾) below, Earth (坤) above, their qi interpenetrating. This is the opposite of their "natural" positions (heaven above, earth below), but it is this VERY reversal that enables generative interaction. The hexagram teaches that hierarchy alone is sterile — only when the high descends and the low ascends does true prosperity emerge.

### 2E) 否 Pǐ — Obstruction / Stagnation (Hexagram 12)

**Structure:** 乾 (Heaven) above, 坤 (Earth) below

> **卦辞:** 否：否之匪人，不利君子贞。大往小来。
> **English:** Pi: Obstruction — not from [true] humanity. Not advantageous for the noble person's correctness. The great departs, the small arrives.

> **《彖传》:** 否之匪人，不利君子贞，大往小来。则是天地不交而万物不通也，上下不交而天下无邦也。内阴而外阳，内柔而外刚，内小人而外君子。小人道长，君子道消也。

> **《大象》:** 天地不交，否。君子以俭德辟难，不可荣以禄。

| Line | Chinese | English | Key Pattern |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| 初六 | 拔茅茹，以其汇，贞吉，亨。 | Pulling up reed-grass — by its connected roots. Correctness: auspicious. Penetrating. | Same image as 泰初九, but here: withdrawing together in times of obstruction |
| 六二 | 包承，小人吉，大人否。亨。 | Embracing acceptance — the petty person: auspicious. The great person: obstructed. Penetrating. | The yin line in the central position: accommodating the obstruction |
| 六三 | 包羞。 | Embracing shame. | Misplaced accommodation leads to disgrace |
| 九四 | 有命，无咎。畴离祉。 | Having the mandate — no blame. The kindred attain blessing. | Yang beginning to act within the obstruction |
| 九五 | 休否，大人吉。其亡其亡，系于苞桑。 | The obstruction ceases — the great person: auspicious. "It is perishing! It is perishing!" — tied to the bushy mulberry. | Vigilance even in the moment of relief |
| 上九 | 倾否。先否后喜。 | The obstruction overturned. First obstruction, then joy. | The breaking of stagnation |

**王弼's 卦主 reading of 否:**

> **Original (Wang Bi's 否卦注, reconstructed):** 「否，闭也。小人道长，君子道消，故不利君子贞。大往小来，上下不交，而天下无邦也。」

> **English:** "Pi means closure. The way of the petty person grows; the way of the noble person recedes. Hence it is not advantageous for the noble person's correctness. The great departs, the small arrives — above and below do not intersect, and the realm has no state [no proper governance]."

**帛书 variant:** The Mawangdui silk manuscript uses **婦** (wife/woman) instead of **否** (obstruction). This is one of the most striking name variants in the entire corpus. If 婦 is the earlier name, the hexagram's core meaning may have originally been about the dynamics of the feminine/yin occupying the upper position — not abstract "obstruction" but a specific social-cosmic configuration. The received text's 否 represents a later philosophical abstraction of this concrete image.

### 2F) 同人 Tóng Rén — Fellowship / Community (Hexagram 13)

**Structure:** 乾 (Heaven) above, 离 (Fire) below

> **卦辞:** 同人：同人于野，亨。利涉大川，利君子贞。
> **English:** Tong Ren: Fellowship in the open country. Penetrating. Advantageous to cross the great river. Advantageous for the noble person's correctness.

> **《彖传》:** 同人，柔得位得中而应乎乾，曰同人。同人曰：同人于野亨，利涉大川，乾行也。文明以健，中正而应，君子正也。唯君子为能通天下之志。

> **《大象》:** 天与火，同人。君子以类族辨物。

| Line | Chinese | English | Key Pattern |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| 初九 | 同人于门，无咎。 | Fellowship at the gate — no blame. | Inclusive from the start |
| 六二 | 同人于宗，吝。 | Fellowship only within the clan — regret. | The single yin (六二) is the卦主 — but clan-only fellowship is too narrow |
| 九三 | 伏戎于莽，升其高陵，三岁不兴。 | Hiding weapons in the thicket, ascending the high hill — three years without rising. | Conflict concealed beneath fellowship |
| 九四 | 乘其墉，弗克攻，吉。 | Mounting their wall — unable to attack. Auspicious. | Restraint in conflict |
| 九五 | 同人，先号咷而后笑，大师克相遇。 | Fellowship — first crying out, then laughing. The great armies meet in victory. | Conflict resolved through fellowship |
| 上九 | 同人于郊，无悔。 | Fellowship in the outskirts — no regret. | Even incomplete fellowship is better than none |

**Structural note:** 同人's single yin line at 六二 is the hexagram-ruler (卦主 by 多寡原则). It is central (得中) and correct (得位 — yin in yin position), and it responds to 九五 (the ruler position). This configuration makes it the most structurally perfect of the single-yin-dominant hexagrams — the image of one principled person drawing others into genuine fellowship.

**Cross-source note:** Both the received text AND the Mawangdui silk manuscript preserve 同人 unchanged — this is one of the most stable hexagram names across all textual traditions, suggesting its meaning was universally recognized.

## 3) Cross-School Contradiction Matrix: Yi Jing Commentary Traditions

**Sources:** Lin Zhongjun 2020 article (agent-tools/d5c351fc-*.txt), Wang Bi scholarly article (agent-tools/42c07f71-*.txt), BATCH_06 Cheng Yi + Zhu Xi extractions

### 3A) Foundational Stance: What IS the Yi Jing?

| Dimension | 王弼 (226–249 CE) | 程颐 (1033–1107 CE) | 朱熹 (1130–1200 CE) |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Nature of the Yi** | A philosophical text disclosing the relationship between 有 (being) and 无 (non-being) through images and words | A vehicle of 天理 (Heavenly Principle) made visible through the hexagrams; "尽天理，斯谓之《易》" | Originally a 卜筮之书 (divination book); principles are embedded WITHIN the oracle framework, not floating above it |
| **Primary framework** | 玄学 (Neo-Daoist metaphysics): 无 as ontological ground of 有 | 理学 (Neo-Confucian): 天理 as the cosmic-moral order | 理学 + 象数: integration of principle (理) with image-number (象数) divination methodology |
| **Role of divination** | Effectively eliminated — the Yi is a book of philosophy, not fortune-telling | Subordinated — "随时变易以从道也" (change according to the time to follow the Dao) | Recovered — "《易》本卜筮之书"; divination is the TEXT'S OWN discourse that must be honored before extracting principles |
| **Source of authority** | 老子 + 《易传》 — Neo-Daoist reading | 孔子 + 四书 — Confucian moral reading through the Four Books | 伏羲/文王/周公/孔子 — four-author historical layering; each author's contribution must be read in its own register |

### 3B) KEY CONTRADICTION: 复卦 "复其见天地之心乎"

This ONE line from the 《彖传》 reveals the deepest philosophical divergence between the three schools.

| | 王弼 | 程颐 | 朱熹 |
|---|------|------|------|
| **天地之心 =** | 无 (Non-Being) / 寂然至无 — the silent, still ontological ground | 生物之心 (the heart that generates life) / 动之端 (the beginning of movement) — the life-giving impulse | 阳复 (the return of yang) — the cyclical resurgence of generative energy |
| **"复" means:** | 反本 (returning to the root of existence) | 阳复于下 (yang returning at the bottom) — cosmic renewal | The divinatory image of one yang line returning — an oracle about timing |
| **Core claim** | Stillness is the ground; movement arises from and returns to stillness | Movement IS the heart — "一阳复于下，乃天地生物之心也" (one yang returning below — THIS is Heaven-Earth's life-generating heart) | The hexagram shows a situation in which return is auspicious |
| **Philosophical lineage** | Laozi §16: "归根曰静" (returning to the root is called stillness) | Confucian vitality: the generative, not the quiescent, is primary | Divination recovery: what matters is what the oracle tells the diviner |
| **Quote from original** | 「寂然至无，是其本矣」— "Silent absolute non-being — this is its root" | 「一阳复于下，乃天地生物之心也」— "One yang returning below — this is Heaven-Earth's life-generating heart" | 「积阴之下，一阳复生。天地生物之心，几于灭息，而至此乃复」— "Beneath accumulated yin, one yang returns to life. Heaven-Earth's life-generating heart, nearly extinguished, here returns" |

**Verdict on this contradiction:** This is NOT a minor interpretive nuance. It is a FUNDAMENTAL metaphysical divergence:
- **Wang Bi:** The ultimate reality is stillness (静/无); activity is derivative
- **Cheng Yi:** The ultimate reality is generative vitality (生); stillness is the interval between life-pulses
- **Zhu Xi:** Don't philosophize first — read the oracle; THEN extract what principle you find

### 3C) KEY CONTRADICTION: 坤卦 and the Status of Yin

| **坤's agency** | 至顺而后乃亨 — yin achieves its purpose THROUGH compliance, not despite it. Compliance IS yin's mode of power | 坤道其顺乎，承天而时行 — Kun's way is compliance, receiving Heaven and acting according to the season | 坤者，顺也，阴之性也 — Kun is compliance, the nature of yin. In divination, the person receiving this hexagram should understand yielding as the correct path |
| **"利牝马之贞"** | The mare is a philosophical symbol of effective receptivity | The mare follows; yin follows yang — this is the natural order | The mare is a divinatory image for concrete guidance in particular situations |
| **Yin's ontological status** | Yin is as fundamental as yang; the soft overcomes the hard (柔能克刚) | Yin serves yang; hierarchy is natural and good | Yin and yang are equal at the cosmological level; in divination, their evaluation depends on position and timing |

### 3D) KEY CONTRADICTION: Method of Reading — How to Extract Meaning

| | 王弼 | 程颐 | 朱熹 |
|---|------|------|------|
| **Primary method** | 得意忘象 — trace words → images → meaning, then release fixation on the lower levels | 体用一源，显微无间 — substance and function share one source; the hidden and manifest have no gap. Image IS principle's visible form. **No "forgetting" of images** | 由卜筮入 → 推演义理 — first understand the hexagram as a divination result, THEN extrapolate the principles |
| **Image-status relationship** | Images are instruments to be transcended once meaning is grasped | Images are NECESSARY manifestations — they cannot be transcended without losing the principle | Images are divination tools that contain principles — both must be preserved |
| **Cheng Yi's direct critique of Wang Bi:** | — | The 体用一源 framework REJECTS the 忘象 move. For Cheng Yi, "forgetting the image" loses the principle because image IS principle's visible body. The metaphor of the snare-and-rabbit (蹄筌) is rejected — the image is not a disposable tool | Implicit critique through method: Zhu Xi criticizes those who "弃卜筮而虚谈义理" (discard divination yet empty-talk principles) — this targets the Wang Bi→Cheng Yi lineage |

### 3F) The Wang→Cheng→Zhu Genealogy: Common Thread Beneath the Differences

Despite the contradictions documented above, scholarly consensus (documented in the two agent-tools articles) identifies a DEEP commonality:

**All three belong to the 义理派 (Meaning-Principle School) of Yi Jing interpretation**, as opposed to the Han 象数派 (Image-Number School) represented by 郑玄, 荀爽, 虞翻.

Their shared commitments:
1. **Internalist reading (内在性):** Meaning comes from the hexagram's OWN structure, not from external celestial-numerological correspondences
2. **Philosophical ambition:** The Yi is read as disclosing fundamental truths about reality, not just as a fortune-telling manual
3. **Anti-numerological excess:** All three reject the proliferation of numerological systems (爻辰, 纳甲, 卦气, etc.) that dominated Han dynasty Yi studies
4. **Human-centered interpretation:** All three read hexagrams through the lens of human affairs, ethics, and governance (全释人事), not through astral-calendrical cycles

**The genealogy:**
- **王弼** established the 义理 paradigm by breaking with Han 象数 externality
- **程颐** inherited the paradigm but replaced its Neo-Daoist metaphysics with Confucian 天理
- **朱熹** inherited both but recovered the divinatory function that the previous two had suppressed, creating a synthesis where divination provides the DISCIPLINED STARTING POINT for philosophical extraction

## 4) Cross-School Contradiction Matrix: Zi Wei Dou Shu

**Sources:** Web-search-confirmed school taxonomy (紫微台 ziwaitai.com, 学易网 xueyizone.com, 田丰源 zwdshu.com)

### 4A) Major Zi Wei School Taxonomy

Zi Wei Dou Shu has no single canonical lineage. The following four major school groups represent genuinely different interpretive traditions:

| School | Key Figure(s) | Period | Core Methodology | Distinctive Position |
|--------|--------------|--------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **中州派 (Zhongzhou)** | 王亭之 (Wang Tingzhi), 陆斌兆 (Lu Binzhao) | 20th c. (roots claim to Tang-Song) | 星曜性质 → 星曜互涉 → 四化影响 → 十二宫流转 (star-nature → star-interaction → four-transformations → palace-transit) | Anti-mechanical; palace essence first; stars read as REACTIONS to palace nature |
| **三合派 (Sanhe / Triplicity School)** | Various, traditional texts | Ming-Qing to present | 星情格局 — emphasis on star-nature patterns and configurations (格局) | Closest to traditional texts 《全书》《全集》; uses star-quality + palace + triplicity as primary, 四化 as secondary |
| **飞星派 (Feixing / Flying Star School)** | 梁湘润 (Liang Xiangrun) and others | 20th c. Taiwan | 四化为核心 — the four transformations (化禄/权/科/忌) are the primary interpretive engine; stars fly between palaces creating dynamic networks | Heavily focused on 四化 飞宫 (flying stars) rather than star-nature qualities |
| **透派 (Tou Pai / Ming-Cheng School)** | Japanese-influenced lineage | 20th c. | Similar to traditional texts but with different 命身宫 assignments; otherwise largely consistent with received tradition | Name from 明澄派; the 命宫/身宫 placement method differs from other schools |

### 4B) Zhongzhou School (王亭之) vs. Other Lineages — Key Methodological Differences

| Dimension | 中州派 (Wang Tingzhi) | 三合派 (Mainstream) | 飞星派 |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Primary interpretive weight** | Balanced: 星曜性质 first, 四化 second, palace context frames everything | 星情 > 四化; triplicity patterns (三方四正) are central | 四化 > 星情; which star transforms to what is more important than the star's innate nature |
| **Palace reading method** | 宫垣本质先行 — establish each of the 12 palaces' intrinsic nature FIRST, then read stars as reactions to that nature | 星入宫 — the star entering the palace: what happens when THIS star occupies THIS palace | 宫干飞化 — the palace's heavenly stem determines which stars fly where; the NETWORK of flying transformations matters most |
| **Anti-mechanical stance** | **Explicitly anti-mechanical** — rejects "固定地移宫换位的缺点" (the flaw of fixed palace-shifting); same star-palace combination means different things in different decade/year contexts | **Moderately formulaic** — star-by-palace catalogues (星在十二宫) serve as reference points, but experienced practitioners adjust for context | **Systematic** — the flying-star methodology is itself a systematic formula, but one based on dynamic transformation rather than static star-palace pairs |
| **Treatment of 四化** | Wang Tingzhi's branch: 庚/戊/壬 天干 四化 assignment DIFFERS from mainstream. This is a significant doctrinal divergence. The Lu Binzhao branch may use more conventional assignments | Follows conventional 四化 tables | Uses 四化 intensively; the entire interpretation revolves around which palace's stem triggers which star's transformation |
| **Number of stars used** | 14 正曜 + 辅曜 + 杂曜 (comprehensive) | Similar comprehensive set | Focus on 14 正曜 + 四化; fewer miscellaneous stars |
| **Philosophical stance** | **Anti-fatalist** — "完全反对宿命与迷信"; Zi Wei is a tool for understanding life's choices, not for predicting fixed destiny | Mixed — traditional texts are more fatalistic; modern practitioners vary | Typically more predictive in tone; the systematic nature lends itself to deterministic readings |

### 4C) Specific Contradiction: 庚/戊/壬 天干 四化 Assignments

Wang Tingzhi's branch of the Zhongzhou school uses a DIFFERENT 四化 star assignment for 庚, 戊, and 壬 heavenly stems compared to mainstream Sanhe and many Feixing practitioners. This is one of the most concrete technical contradictions:

| Heavenly Stem | Mainstream 四化 | Wang Tingzhi 四化 (reported) | Impact |
|---------------|----------------|------------------------------|--------|
| 庚 (Geng) | 太阳化禄, 武曲化权, 太阴化科, 天同化忌 | May differ for 化科/化忌 assignment | Changes which stars transform in Geng-year charts |
| 戊 (Wu) | 贪狼化禄, 太阴化权, 右弼化科, 天机化忌 | May differ | Affects Wu-year chart interpretation |
| 壬 (Ren) | 天梁化禄, 紫微化权, 左辅化科, 武曲化忌 | May differ | Changes financial/career readings for Ren-year natives |

**Significance:** This means two qualified practitioners using different schools could derive OPPOSITE conclusions about the same chart if they disagree on which star transforms to忌 (taboo/misfortune) vs. 禄 (prosperity) for a given year.

### 4D) Wang Tingzhi vs. Lu Binzhao — Internal Zhongzhou School Differences

Though both are classified as "中州派," the two branches have distinct characteristics:

| | 王亭之 branch | 陆斌兆 branch |
|---|-------------|-------------|
| **Core text** | 《中州派紫微斗数深造讲义》(original lectures) | 《紫微斗数讲义》(with Wang Tingzhi's annotations) |
| **Method emphasis** | Star-interaction (星曜互涉) and 60-configuration system — deeply original | 14-star × 12-palace drill matrix — closer to traditional pedagogical format |
| **四化 usage** | More innovative; "比传统更丰富多彩" (more varied than tradition) | More conservative; closer to standard 四化 tables |
| **流年 method** | "别树一格" (distinctive approach) — year-flow reading is a signature innovation | More conventional year-flow methods |
| **Transmission** | Wang learned from 刘惠苍; published openly | Lu's lineage had wider transmission through students |
